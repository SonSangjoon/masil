# Long-term vision

> **MASIL begins by reconnecting Korean elders with creative life. Only if that experience proves
> useful and trusted should it become a bridge to human support.**

Everything beyond the current calligraphy and Janggi experience is a future hypothesis. MASIL has
not established institutional adoption, reduced social isolation, saved staff time, or prevented
`godoksa`—dying alone and remaining undiscovered.

## Why the vision begins with creativity

Many products approach Korean elders living alone first as people to monitor, assess, or process.
MASIL starts from a different relationship: the person comes to create, play, practice, improve,
and make decisions.

That order is a product boundary:

- MASIL must provide value even if no support need ever appears.
- The elder remains a maker and player, not a passive subject of risk detection.
- Trust must arise from repeated useful activity, not invisible observation.
- Any future request for help must begin with the elder's words and explicit choice.

Calligraphy and Janggi are therefore more than demonstration features. They may become an ordinary,
voluntary reason for a homebound Korean elder to return, participate, and speak.

## The social hypothesis

Korean elders living alone can face overlapping barriers: limited mobility, digital exclusion, and
reduced access to recurring social spaces. Some needs may remain invisible until they become urgent,
while human care systems operate with finite attention.

MASIL's long-term hypothesis is deliberately conditional:

> If a creative Agent–WebMCP experience becomes a trusted part of everyday life, an elder who
> chooses to ask for help may be able to express that need earlier and carry it into a human
> conversation without starting from zero.

This is not automatic detection. Silence, absence, handwriting, gameplay, camera movement, voice
tone, or conversational sentiment must never become a hidden welfare signal.

## A staged path

### Horizon 0 — verify the creative product

MASIL must first prove that the current product works technically and is worthwhile for the intended
audience. The Agent's contribution must become correct, visible, shapeable activity; human
authorship and direct action must remain consequential; and familiar Korean must become valid
Janggi play without interface instruction.

A connected demo is necessary, but it does not establish accessibility or sustained use. The
technical gate is defined in [Evaluation](EVALUATION.md).

### Horizon 1 — run one bounded creative pilot

The first real-world pilot remains entirely within creative participation. It should involve one
senior welfare center, cultural program, or community organization; a small group of participating
homebound Korean elders; one culturally knowledgeable facilitator; and one technical owner.

The pilot asks whether participants can begin and resume without interface instruction, understand
what the Agent contributed, correct it in familiar language, retain authorship, and choose to
return. Expansion stops if the experience is confusing, patronizing, difficult to correct, or
valuable only during guided testing.

### Horizon 2 — test one person-initiated support path

Only after the creative experience earns trust should MASIL test a separate support path:

1. The elder explicitly asks for help in their own words.
2. Their Agent helps clarify the intended meaning and outcome.
3. WebMCP places a concise, visible, correctable request into an authorized provider page.
4. The elder reviews what would be shared and decides whether to proceed.
5. A participating human organization receives the approved request.
6. Human staff retain assessment, eligibility, exceptions, decisions, contact, and care.

The current local experimental tools do not implement this service. They contact no institution,
hold no real identity or queue, and demonstrate no care outcome.

## Why finite human capacity matters

Korea's customized-care system reports serving about **550,000 vulnerable elders** through roughly
**2,600 dedicated social workers and 35,000 frontline care workers**. These figures establish scale;
they do not prove a nationwide staffing shortage or MASIL's ability to save time.

Sources:

- [Customized Care Service for Older Adults, Ministry of Health and Welfare](https://www.mohw.go.kr/menu.es?mid=a10712010400)
- [National workforce and recipient figures, Central Support Center for Older Adults Living Alone](https://www.1661-2129.or.kr/sub05/sub01/sub01.php)
- [Integrated welfare delivery and case management, Ministry of Health and Welfare](https://www.mohw.go.kr/menu.es?mid=a10708040100)

The testable bottleneck lies before professional care: repeated explanation, clarification, missing
information, routing, and status follow-up. Agent interpretation and WebMCP continuity may help
preserve context so scarce human attention remains focused on judgment, trust, exceptions, and
presence. That is a provider hypothesis, not a current benefit claim.

A 2025 peer-reviewed Korean case series illustrates the kind of break this future work would need
to study. An 80-year-old man living alone documented his situation with help, but the service
linkage stopped when the local office still required an in-person visit he found difficult. The case
does not establish frequency or prove MASIL would have solved it; it shows how a recognized need can
still fail to reach completed human support.

Source: [Resident-Led Integrated Health and Welfare Case Management in Depopulated Areas, 2025](https://www.kihasa.re.kr/hswr/assets/pdf/1560/journal-45-2-78.pdf)

## Requirements before any institutional pilot

No real support integration begins without:

1. target-user evidence that the creative product is useful on its own;
2. a named, authorized partner with an accountable workflow owner;
3. identity, authentication, data minimization, retention, deletion, and audit rules;
4. review and consent that work for the intended audience;
5. a truthful human response promise and escalation boundary;
6. legal, privacy, accessibility, and safeguarding review; and
7. recovery that never leaves an elder believing help is on the way when it is not.

## Stop conditions

MASIL must stop or narrow expansion if:

- creative activity becomes a pretext for monitoring;
- the Agent initiates support without an explicit request;
- people cannot distinguish Agent suggestions from institutional decisions;
- a partner cannot provide a truthful, visible next step;
- the workflow increases repeated explanation or staff burden;
- participants feel less in control; or
- a simpler accessible interface can deliver the same outcome without Agent or WebMCP.

## Broader accessibility direction

MASIL's larger ambition is not to become one portal for every service. It is to demonstrate a
pattern other websites can adopt: each website exposes its real capabilities and boundaries through
WebMCP; the person's Agent carries language and private context; the website remains authoritative;
and the person can see, shape, correct, interrupt, and resume the result.

The question for an Agent-native open web is:

> **Can a person participate meaningfully through their Agent even when they cannot first master
> each website's interface?**

MASIL's first promise remains concrete: bring creative life back within reach and expand it through
the elder's Agent. Its larger vision remains conditional: only after that return path earns trust
may it become an earlier, person-controlled bridge to finite human care.
