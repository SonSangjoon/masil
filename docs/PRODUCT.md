# Product definition

> **MASIL turns Agent access into human participation, allowing Korean elders to resume and expand
> meaningful creative activity without first mastering the web interface.**

This document defines the current product: two culturally grounded creative experiences that prove
one Agent-native accessibility model. It does not define a welfare service, institutional handoff,
or care workflow. Those possibilities are isolated in [Long-term vision](VISION.md).

## The target moment

MASIL is designed around a situation, not an age label:

> A Korean elder living alone can no longer reach a familiar creative or social space. A web
> alternative exists, but entering it requires menus, modes, coordinates, and controls they have
> never needed before.

The elder may be comfortable saying what they want in everyday Korean, looking at a screen, and
making direct choices while remaining uncomfortable operating a conventional website. MASIL
addresses that mismatch without treating age or household type as a deficit score.

The target does not imply that every Korean elder lives alone, has limited mobility or digital
capability, wants the same activities, or needs monitoring or intervention.

## The product problem

Conventional accessibility improves the interface presented to a person. That work remains
essential, but it still often assumes that the person can understand the interface well enough to
begin.

An Agent can understand language, generate content, and make decisions. On its own, however, its
contribution can end as an answer or file outside the activity. A strong fixed UI can hold valid
state, but it cannot anticipate every creative request or understand the language a person has used
for decades.

MASIL joins those incomplete sides. The elder starts from the activity they want. Their Agent
creates or interprets. WebMCP turns that contribution into a live website activity, allowing the
elder to begin without mastering the interface and continue in the same creative space.

The product is not voice control for a website. It removes interface mastery as a prerequisite for
meaningful participation.

## The product promise

> **The Agent expands what is possible. WebMCP turns that capability into a live activity the
> elder can enter and continue without first learning the interface.**

A successful MASIL experience lets a Korean elder:

- begin from a creative intention expressed in familiar language;
- receive an Agent contribution inside the correct live activity;
- inspect, change, or continue that contribution directly;
- turn that contribution into an activity they can actively continue; and
- let their own action influence what the Agent does next.

The result is not faster navigation. It is a meaningful activity restored and expanded beyond what
a fixed library or interface could predefine.

## Two complementary product capabilities

### Generative calligraphy

Each phrase and brush style can require a new reference. The teacher who once prepared it may no
longer be within reach, while a fixed library cannot anticipate every word, occasion, or aesthetic
choice.

MASIL lets the Agent create the exact requested reference and place it inside the live calligraphy
space without overwriting the elder's work. The Agent expands the available starting points; the
elder determines what the work becomes.

### Conversational Janggi

Experience across a real Janggi table is expressed through relationships between pieces and the
shorthand of live play—not coordinates, piece IDs, or menu structure.

MASIL lets the Agent understand that familiar language against the website's current, valid game
state. The website preserves the full rules, the Agent can take the other side, and the elder can
continue the same match in words or directly on the board.

Calligraphy demonstrates generative expansion inside a continuing activity. Janggi demonstrates
grounded interpretation and continuity while preserving the game's structure. They are localized
proofs, not claims that every Korean elder shares the same preferences.

## Responsibilities

| Participant | Product responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Korean elder | Creative intention, brushstrokes, game choices, correction, interruption, and final control |
| Elder's Agent | Conversation, private context, interpretation, generation, and next-action judgment |
| MASIL | Live creative state, visible results, rules, valid actions, recovery, and execution truth |
| WebMCP | Semantic, bidirectional connection between the Agent and MASIL's provider-owned activity |

No participant substitutes for another. Agent confidence cannot become provider truth, and Agent
output does not become human participation simply because it appears on screen.

## Product principles

1. **Participation before operation.** Begin from what the person wants to do, not from knowledge of
   the interface.
2. **Full activity, not a simplified imitation.** Preserve the full creative practice and complete
   Janggi rules.
3. **Generation that remains shapeable.** Agent output matters when the elder can continue from it.
4. **Provider truth.** The website owns valid state, rules, and visible results.
5. **Bidirectional continuity.** The Agent can change the activity, and the elder's action can change
   the Agent's next decision.
6. **One existing Agent.** MASIL does not embed a competing conversational model.
7. **Cultural specificity without stereotyping.** The activities are grounded Korean cases, not a
   universal claim about age.
8. **Evidence before claims.** Technical completion and real-world accessibility are separate
   standards.

## Current success boundary

The current product case succeeds only when the requested calligraphy or Janggi activity becomes
correct, visible, shapeable, and continuous. A chat answer, generated file, tool call, static
animation, or Computer Use recreation is not the same result.

Technical verification is defined in [Evaluation](EVALUATION.md). Whether the experience is truly
accessible and worthwhile for Korean elders requires later target-user research.

## Non-goals

Current MASIL does not:

- replace a community center, teacher, friend, or human relationship;
- monitor silence, absence, voice, handwriting, camera frames, or gameplay for risk;
- infer loneliness, cognition, health, or welfare need;
- perform welfare intake, institutional routing, or an external handoff;
- claim measured improvement in digital capability or social isolation;
- assume every Korean elder wants the same activities; or
- present an evaluation or deployed journey as verified before its evidence is frozen.
