# Evidence and Korean context

> Evidence review date: **2026-09-02**

MASIL is culturally specific, but its claims must remain auditable to someone
who does not know Korea. This document separates observed evidence, product
interpretation, and unproven hypotheses.

The evidence does **not** say that every older Korean living alone is lonely,
mobility-limited, digitally excluded, or interested in calligraphy and Janggi.
It establishes that the chosen target and failure moments are real enough to
design and test against.

## Evidence standard

Sources are used in this order:

1. Korean national statistics and current ministry policy;
2. peer-reviewed or public research using national data;
3. current local-government program material; and
4. journalism for lived cultural context that official tables do not capture.

Each claim records what the source supports and what it does not prove.

## 1. A large audience, without turning household type into a diagnosis

Korea entered a super-aged demographic structure: the national statistics
office reported that people aged 65 or older represented **20.3%** of the
population in 2025. The more product-specific number is the 2024 census count
of **2.289 million older one-person households**.

Sources:

- [2025 Statistics on Older Persons, Statistics Korea](https://kostat.go.kr/board.es?act=view&bid=10820&list_no=438832&mid=a10301060100&ref_bid=218%2C219%2C220%2C10820%2C11815%2C11895%2C11816%2C208%2C0067&tag=)
- [2024 Population and Housing Census results, Statistics Korea](https://www.kostat.go.kr/boardDownload.es?bid=203&list_no=437767&seq=3)

MASIL intentionally says “older adults living alone,” not “lonely seniors.” A
one-person household tells us who is not continuously present in the home. It
does not tell us the person's relationships, competence, preference, or risk.

## 2. The missing layer is between access and agency

The 2025 national digital-divide report offers the clearest product signal. On
an index where the general public equals 100, older adults reached **95.4** in
access but only **56.2** in capability. Their raw capability score was **37.8
out of 100**, compared with **65.4** for the general public.

The 2023 national older-adult survey found smartphone ownership had risen from
56.4% in 2020 to **76.6%** in 2023, while computer ownership reached 20.6%.
Taken together, these sources support a bounded conclusion:

> Connected devices are reaching more older adults than the ability to use the
> interfaces built on top of them.

Sources:

- [2025 Digital Divide Survey, National Information Society Agency](https://www.nia.or.kr/site/nia_kor/ex/bbs/View.do?bcIdx=29168&cbIdx=81623)
- [2023 National Survey of Older Persons results, Ministry of Health and Welfare](https://www.mohw.go.kr/board.es?act=view&bid=0027&list_no=1483352&mid=a10503010100&nPage=2)

This is why MASIL does not stop at bigger text or voice commands. The page must
tell the person's Agent exactly what it can do, and the Agent must turn those
capabilities into a visible, reversible experience.

## 3. Mobility creates a double exclusion

The long product story targets people who face two barriers at once:

1. a familiar offline activity or service is difficult to reach; and
2. the web alternative is difficult to operate.

The national survey analysis found 56.6% of respondents selected a main
difficulty when going out. Stairs and slopes, boarding a bus or train, limited
transport, unsafe road conditions, and inaccessible transport facilities all
appeared. It also found a pronounced walking-distance gap to welfare centers.

Source:

- [“Older Adults' Living Environment and Perception of Later Life,” KIHASA, based on the 2023 National Survey of Older Persons](https://repository.kihasa.re.kr/bitstream/201002/46105/1/2024.11.No.337.06.pdf)

MASIL does not claim that a remote activity replaces an accessible
neighborhood. It asks what can remain available when the neighborhood is
temporarily or persistently out of reach.

## 4. Why calligraphy and Janggi

The 2025 national senior-welfare program guidance lists calligraphy under
artistic activities and Janggi under cultural activities in the example
hobby-and-leisure program for senior welfare centers. MASIL therefore does not
join an arbitrary art feature to an arbitrary game. Both are grounded in the
creative and social activities already associated with older adults' community
life.

Source:

- [2025 Senior Health and Welfare Program Guidance, Volume 1](https://www.kcpass.or.kr/fileDownload?fileDownType=C&fileId=3&paramMenuId=MENU002030702000000&titleId=9vRotzVdm8)

### Calligraphy: a generative creative medium

Current local program guides show calligraphy alongside the kinds of classes
people encounter at Korean neighborhood administrative welfare centers. MASIL
uses that recognizable form to prove something a fixed catalog cannot: the
person can request a phrase that does not yet exist, the Agent can generate it,
and WebMCP can place it into the exact live canvas without overwriting the
person's own strokes.

Source:

- [2025 Saha-gu Lifelong Learning and Neighborhood Program Guide](https://health.saha.go.kr/edu/Downfiles/sahaedu_info_202509.pdf)

### Janggi: strategy as a social creative scene

Janggi is Korean chess. At Tapgol Park in Seoul, outdoor boards served for
decades as a low-cost place to play, watch, argue about moves, and talk. Their
2025 removal was publicly debated because removing disorder also removed a
gathering surface. Later reporting found older players had dispersed and
described the boards as a form of self-organized social culture.

Sources:

- [Yonhap: Tapgol Park's Janggi boards and older adults' changing destinations](https://www.yna.co.kr/view/AKR20250912127400518)
- [JTBC: the 30-year Janggi venue and the loss of an affordable place to gather](https://v.daum.net/v/20250825200046781)
- [Hankook Ilbo: follow-up reporting on the dispersed Tapgol community](https://www.hankookilbo.com/news/article/A2026011018500002067)

The evidence does not justify nostalgia as product strategy. It justifies
building the board as a conversational social object rather than a menu-driven
game.

## 5. The support gap is between detection and completed linkage

Korea already has systems for safety checks, AI-assisted check-ins, care visits,
and public-service discovery. MASIL does not need to duplicate an inactivity
sensor. The more relevant failure can happen after someone has spoken, a need
has been recognized, and paperwork exists.

The 2025 case series is unusually concrete. An 80-year-old participant living
alone had mobility difficulty and hearing loss. With help, he wrote his property
and housing situation down. A village care manager delivered the signed
material, but the local office still required him to appear in person. He was
reluctant to visit, and the resource linkage did not continue.

Source:

- [“Resident-Led Integrated Health and Welfare Case Management in Depopulated Areas,” *Health and Social Welfare Review* 45(2), 2025](https://www.kihasa.re.kr/hswr/assets/pdf/1560/journal-45-2-78.pdf)

MASIL cannot override an agency's attendance requirement. It can make the
boundary legible, preserve the person's approved account, prepare the next
human conversation, return exact status, and avoid making the person start from
zero.

## 6. The provider opportunity is administrative repetition, not human removal

The national customized-care system reported about **550,000 vulnerable older
adults**, **2,600 dedicated social workers**, and **35,000 frontline care
workers** as of August 2025. These figures show the scale of the service, not a
measured nationwide staff shortage.

The Ministry's current customized-care service covers safety, social
participation, education, daily-life support, specialized support, and resource
linkage. Its sequence crosses a neighborhood office, a commissioned service
provider, municipal decision, service worker, reassessment, and follow-up.

The wider integrated case-management model includes discovery, initial
counseling, intake, needs and risk assessment, case meetings, selection, service
planning, delivery and monitoring, closure, and follow-up. The same Ministry
page states an appropriate caseload of around 15 households for one case
manager.

Sources:

- [Customized Care Service for Older Adults, Ministry of Health and Welfare](https://www.mohw.go.kr/menu.es?mid=a10712010400)
- [National customized-care workforce and recipient figures, Central Support Center for Older Adults Living Alone](https://www.1661-2129.or.kr/sub05/sub01/sub01.php)
- [Integrated welfare delivery and case management, Ministry of Health and Welfare](https://www.mohw.go.kr/menu.es?mid=a10708040100)

MASIL's provider hypothesis is deliberately narrow. A page-owned, revisioned,
person-approved work object may reduce time spent on repeated explanation,
manual transcription, missing-information follow-up, scheduling, and status
calls. Human staff still own assessment, exceptions, contact, and official
decisions.

## What the evidence does not prove

The repository must not claim that MASIL has already:

- reduced loneliness, isolation, or solitary-death risk;
- increased cultural participation;
- improved digital capability;
- saved public-sector labor or money;
- completed a public-service application;
- been tested with the target population;
- been adopted by a community center, municipality, or care provider; or
- established calligraphy or Janggi as universal preferences.

Those are research or pilot questions. The completed challenge demo
demonstrates a new interaction capability and a credible path to evaluate
it—not the social outcome itself.

The research design and expansion criteria for those questions are kept in
[Long-term vision](VISION.md).
