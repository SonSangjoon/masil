# Evidence and Korean context

> Evidence review date: **2026-09-03**

MASIL is culturally specific, but its claims must remain understandable and auditable to someone
who does not know Korea. This document supports the current creative product only:

- why Korean elders living alone form a meaningful target context;
- why device access does not equal the ability to participate;
- why mobility can close both physical and digital routes; and
- why calligraphy and Janggi are grounded product choices.

Evidence about a possible future institutional or human-support bridge belongs in
[Long-term vision](VISION.md), not in the current product case.

## Evidence standard

Sources are used in this order:

1. Korean national statistics and current ministry policy;
2. peer-reviewed or public research using national data;
3. current local-government program material; and
4. journalism for lived cultural context that official tables do not capture.

Every claim separates four things:

| Layer | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Evidence | What does the source directly establish? |
| Interpretation | Why does that fact matter to MASIL? |
| Design implication | What should change in the product because of it? |
| Limit | What must MASIL not claim from that evidence? |

## 1. A specific audience without a deficit label

Korea entered a super-aged demographic structure in 2025: people aged 65 or older represented
**20.3% of the population**. The more product-specific figure is the 2024 census count of **2.289
million one-person households** in that age group.

Sources:

- [2025 Statistics on Older Persons, Ministry of Data and Statistics](https://www.mods.go.kr/board.es?act=view&bid=10820&list_no=438832&mid=a10301010000)
- [2024 Population and Housing Census results, Statistics Korea](https://www.kostat.go.kr/boardDownload.es?bid=203&list_no=437767&seq=3)

MASIL intentionally says `Korean elders living alone`, not `lonely seniors`. A one-person household
shows who is not continuously present in the home. It does not establish the person's relationships,
competence, health, preference, or risk.

The target is a situation: an elder living alone whose familiar creative place has become difficult
to reach and whose web alternative remains difficult to operate.

## 2. The gap between access and participation

The 2025 national digital-divide survey offers the clearest product signal. On an index where the
general population equals 100, the older-adult group reached **95.4 for access but only 56.2 for
capability**. Its raw capability score was **37.8 out of 100**, compared with **65.4** for the
general population.

The 2023 National Survey of Older Persons separately found smartphone ownership had risen from
56.4% in 2020 to **76.6% in 2023**, while computer ownership reached 20.6%.

Sources:

- [2025 Digital Divide Survey, National Information Society Agency](https://www.nia.or.kr/site/nia_kor/ex/bbs/View.do?bcIdx=29168&cbIdx=81623)
- [2023 National Survey of Older Persons results, Ministry of Health and Welfare](https://www.mohw.go.kr/board.es?act=view&bid=0027&list_no=1483352&mid=a10503010100&nPage=2)

Together, these sources support one bounded conclusion:

> Digital connection is reaching more Korean elders than the ability to use the interfaces built on
> top of it.

This is why MASIL does not stop at voice commands or enlarged controls. WebMCP lets the website
expose exact capabilities and state to the Agent, allowing the person to participate through the
language and knowledge they already have.

The sources do not prove that MASIL solves the gap. That requires direct target-user research and
task-level usability evidence.

## 3. Mobility can create a double exclusion

The product story concerns two barriers occurring together:

1. a familiar offline activity becomes difficult or impossible to reach; and
2. its web alternative is difficult to operate.

Analysis of the national survey found that 56.6% of respondents selected a main difficulty when
going out. Stairs and slopes, boarding public transport, limited transport, unsafe road conditions,
and inaccessible transport facilities all appeared. It also found a pronounced walking-distance
gap to welfare centers.

Source:

- [“Older Adults' Living Environment and Perception of Later Life,” KIHASA, based on the 2023 National Survey of Older Persons](https://repository.kihasa.re.kr/bitstream/201002/46105/1/2024.11.No.337.06.pdf)

MASIL does not argue that a website replaces an accessible neighborhood, a teacher, or a gathering
place. It asks what meaningful participation can remain available when the physical route is closed.

## 4. Why calligraphy and Janggi

The 2025 national senior-welfare program guidance lists calligraphy under artistic activities and
Janggi under cultural activities in its example hobby-and-leisure programs. They are therefore
grounded examples of Korean creative and social life, not an arbitrary art-and-game bundle.

Source:

- [2025 Senior Health and Welfare Program Guidance, Volume 1](https://www.kcpass.or.kr/fileDownload?fileDownType=C&fileId=2&paramMenuId=MENU002030702000000&titleId=tAlhPvqA4H)

### Calligraphy: individualized creative practice

A 2025 local program guide shows calligraphy alongside other classes offered through Korean
neighborhood administrative welfare centers. MASIL uses this familiar form to demonstrate a new
capability: the elder can request a phrase and style that do not yet exist, the Agent can generate a
reference, and WebMCP can place it inside the actual canvas without overwriting human strokes.

Source:

- [2025 Saha-gu Lifelong Learning and Neighborhood Program Guide](https://health.saha.go.kr/edu/Downfiles/sahaedu_info_202509.pdf)

The evidence supports calligraphy's presence in local program culture. It does not prove that every
elder wants calligraphy or that an Agent can replace a teacher.

### Janggi: a conversational social object

At Tapgol Park in Seoul, outdoor boards served for decades as a low-cost place to play, watch, argue
about moves, exchange quick advice, and talk. Their 2025 removal was publicly debated because
removing disorder also removed a gathering surface. Later reporting described the players as
dispersed.

Sources:

- [Yonhap: Tapgol Park's Janggi boards and older adults' changing destinations](https://www.yna.co.kr/view/AKR20250912127400518)
- [JTBC: the 30-year Janggi venue and the loss of an affordable place to gather](https://v.daum.net/v/20250825200046781)
- [Hankook Ilbo: follow-up reporting on the dispersed Tapgol community](https://www.hankookilbo.com/news/article/A2026011018500002067)

This supports designing Janggi as a continuing exchange in familiar language. It does not establish
that remote play recreates the social value of Tapgol Park or reduces isolation.

## Design implications

The combined evidence justifies testing these product decisions:

- target a concrete double-exclusion moment rather than a generic senior persona;
- let familiar language begin the activity without requiring interface translation;
- keep the Web UI as a rich creative space rather than reducing it to simplified controls;
- use Agent generation to expand possible calligraphy material;
- use provider-owned rules and state to preserve the full structure of Janggi; and
- keep the elder as maker, player, and decision-maker.

These are design hypotheses grounded in evidence. They are not measured outcomes.

## What the evidence does not prove

The repository must not claim that MASIL has already:

- increased creative or cultural participation;
- improved digital capability;
- reduced loneliness, social isolation, or solitary-death risk;
- replaced a teacher, community center, or gathering place;
- been tested successfully with the target population;
- established calligraphy or Janggi as universal preferences;
- been adopted by a partner, municipality, or care provider; or
- completed the final deployed WebMCP judge journey.

Technical task success is governed by [Evaluation](EVALUATION.md); human outcomes require separate
target-user tests or pilots. Future social and institutional hypotheses are isolated in
[Long-term vision](VISION.md).
